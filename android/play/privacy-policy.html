DLNE app privacy policy

Effective [DATE OF PUBLICATION]. Applies to the DLNE app for Android (package io.dlne.chat), published by [DEVELOPER NAME AS SHOWN ON GOOGLE PLAY].

In short

  • No accounts. The app never asks for your name, email address or phone number.
  • No ads, no analytics, no crash-reporting services, no advertising identifiers and no tracking of any kind. The app does not use Google Play Services.
  • Your chats are stored only on your phone.
  • When you send a message, the conversation is sent over an encrypted connection to the DLNE service, and an answer is written by AI on DLNE's own server. Only if that server is restarting or down, a computer that someone lends to DLNE may write it instead.
  • Those computers belong to other people. Don't send passwords, private details or anything secret.
  • Photos you add go to DLNE's own server to write the answer and are never sent to a lent computer. Like every message, they are processed by the shared model, whose layers can run on lent graphics cards, which see only intermediate numbers, never the picture. DLNE doesn't keep them. They stay on your phone with the chat.
  • Lookups (weather, Wikipedia, news, web search) are made by the DLNE server, not your phone; reported answers are kept on the server for review.
  • Location is off unless you turn it on (see below).

What stays on your phone

Your conversations (the messages you write and the answers you receive, with the photos you added and the pictures made for you) and two settings: the server address and whether you have read the first-run notice. They are kept in the app's private storage, are excluded from Android cloud backup and device-to-device transfer, and are deleted when you delete a chat, use Settings > Delete all chats, or uninstall the app.

What leaves your phone, and why

Your conversation, when you press Send. The app sends the recent part of the open conversation (up to about 12,000 characters: your messages and the previous answers) to the DLNE service over HTTPS, so that an answer can be written. Nothing is sent until you press Send.

A random conversation number. Each chat gets a random number when it starts, and every message of that chat carries it, so the service can keep a private speed-up cache per chat. It is not tied to you, an account or your phone, it is not part of a report, and a new chat gets a new one.

Photos you add. The + button next to the message box can add up to 4 photos to a message, from Android's photo picker or its camera app (the app asks for no storage or camera permission and sees only the photos you choose). Before a photo is sent, the app re-encodes it as a smaller JPEG, which removes the camera details and the GPS position stored in it. The photos go with that message over HTTPS to the DLNE service, which passes them only to DLNE's own computer to write the answer. While that is your newest message with photos, they go again with the messages you send after it, so the AI can still see them; a newer message with photos replaces them, and photos the service refused are not sent again. They are never sent to a lent computer, and DLNE doesn't keep them. Like every message, a photo is processed by the shared model on DLNE's own server, whose layers can run on lent graphics cards; those cards see only intermediate numbers, never the picture. Photos stay in the app's private storage with the chat and are deleted with it. The server counts the photos a network sends each hour to keep the service fair, using a hash of each photo that is held in memory only. Pictures you ask for (only if the server offers them). If the server has image generation on, you can ask the chat for a picture in your own words, or tap Create image to choose a size. Either way the description (the one you wrote, or the one the chat model writes from your request) and the conversation number go to the DLNE service, which checks the request and the result, makes the picture on DLNE's own computer and holds it in memory for up to 30 minutes after it is made, so the app can collect it, then drops it. The picture is kept in the app's private storage with that chat and deleted with it; it is never sent back to the chat model, only its description is.

A status check. While the app is open it asks the service, about once a minute, which model is in use and how many machines are serving. This request contains no personal information.

A report, only if you send one. See Reporting an answer below.

Your network address. Like any internet service, the DLNE service sees the IP address your request comes from. It uses it only to share the service fairly: one answer at a time and a few requests per minute for each network. [OWNER: CONFIRM that the address is kept only in memory for this purpose and is not logged or stored.]

Update checks (only in the version you download from the DLNE website). The Google Play version never does this; Google Play updates it. The version downloaded as an APK from dlne.rossgilsonlabs.dev asks that website, when the app opens and at most once every 12 hours, for a small file (/downloads/android.json) that names the newest version. It is always the DLNE website, even if you changed the server address. The request contains no personal information and no identifier; like any web request, the website sees your network address. You can switch this off in Settings > Check for updates automatically. If a newer version exists and you tap Update, the app downloads it from the same website, checks that it is exactly the file the website published and that it is signed by the same publisher as the app, and hands it to Android's installer, which asks you to confirm. Nothing is installed without your tap.

Who processes your messages

The DLNE service passes each conversation to DLNE's own server, which runs an open-source language model, writes the answer and streams it back to you through the service. Computers that people lend to DLNE are the backup: only while DLNE's own server is restarting or down, and only if one is available, the service may pass the conversation to one of them instead. A lent computer is owned and run by a member of the public, not by DLNE; it never receives a photo. The DLNE software does not save the text of your messages, but the owner controls a lent computer, so treat anything you send as something the owner of such a computer could see. Answers can be slow and can be wrong.

Lookups: weather, Wikipedia, news and web search

To answer questions about current things, the model can ask the DLNE server to look something up: the weather (Open-Meteo), an article on Wikipedia, the news (GDELT) or a web search (through a SearXNG search service). These lookups are made by the DLNE server, not your phone. Your phone never contacts these services, and none of them receives your phone's network address, your conversation or any identifier from your phone. The server sends them only the short lookup text the model writes, such as a city name or a topic (and, only if you turned location on, coordinates rounded to about 100 m for local weather, or a nearby place name for a local search; see below). They see the DLNE server's network address, as any website would.

The pages found are listed under the answer as sources. Tapping a source opens it in your web browser, and that website then sees your visit like any other.

Location (only if you turn it on)

It is off unless you switch on Settings > Share location, or say yes when the chat asks “Use your location for this?” (a card shown above the message box when a question needs it; it asks at most once in a chat), and allow Android's location permission. You may allow precise or only approximate location; approximate works too, just less exactly. When it is on, your phone's precise location is sent over HTTPS to the DLNE server with a question that looks local (for example one that says “near me” or “nearby”, or asks about the weather), and is used only for lookups that need it. It is not sent with other questions, and never when the only position the phone has is older than two minutes; when a local question goes without it, the app says so under the answer. For local weather, the coordinates, rounded to about 100 m, go from the DLNE server to the weather service (Open-Meteo). For local searches, only a nearby place name is added to the search, which the DLNE server sends to web search engines. The AI model that writes the answer never gets your coordinates. Answers are written on DLNE's own server, with a lent computer as the backup while that server is restarting or down; when a local lookup runs, the results the model reads name the nearby town, so whichever machine answers sees that town's name, but never the coordinates. The DLNE server does not log or store your coordinates. The town can appear in an answer, so if you report that answer, the report (which keeps the answer's text) can name it. Switch it off at any time; nothing is read in the background.

Reporting an answer

You can report any answer that is harmful, wrong or offensive from inside the app. A report contains that answer, the question it answered, the reason you choose and the note you may add (up to 500 characters). Nothing else from your chats, and no account or device identifier, is sent. Reported answers are kept on the DLNE server so the DLNE team can review them and improve the service.

If you change the server

Unless you change it, the app connects to the public DLNE service at https://dlne.rossgilsonlabs.dev. Settings lets you point the app at a different DLNE server, for example one you run yourself. Your messages then go to that server instead, and its operator's practices apply. The app only connects to servers over HTTPS.

Links

Links and sources in answers, and the links in Settings and the chat list (the DLNE website, Discord, source code, Buy Me a Coffee, the DLNE extension on Open VSX), open in your web browser only when you tap them. Those websites have their own privacy policies.

Children

The app is not directed at children. Answers are generated by AI and are not filtered for young audiences.

Security

All traffic between the app and the service is encrypted with HTTPS; the app refuses unencrypted connections. The Google Play version asks for internet access and, only when you turn on location sharing, Android's precise and approximate location permissions. When you save a generated image, Android stores it in your Pictures folder without any storage permission. The version downloaded from the DLNE website also asks to be allowed to install apps (REQUEST_INSTALL_PACKAGES), which it uses only to install a DLNE update after you tap Update.

Your choices

  • Delete any chat from the chat list, or every chat from Settings.
  • Don't include personal or confidential information in your messages.
  • Because there are no accounts, the service holds no profile of you to delete. For any question about this policy, contact us below.
